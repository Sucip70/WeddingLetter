# Cultural geometry review

The Phase 2 geometry uses a restrained, abstracted Javanese vocabulary. It does not reproduce a named restricted court cloth.

## Parang

The edge bands use a diagonal sequence of curved blade/wave forms. This follows the defining diagonal and curved rhythm described by the Yogyakarta Palace while avoiding direct reproduction of a specific `parang rusak` rank variant. Parang remains limited to opposing edges rather than covering the invitation.

Source: [Yogyakarta Palace — Motif Batik Larangan Keraton Yogyakarta](https://www.kratonjogja.id/en/kagungan-dalem/12-motif-batik-larangan-keraton-yogyakarta/)

## Kawung

Each divider unit consists of four ellipses surrounding one center, matching the Palace description of kawung geometry. It appears only as a divider and never as an all-over field.

Source: [Yogyakarta Palace — Kawung description](https://www.kratonjogja.id/en/kagungan-dalem/12-motif-batik-larangan-keraton-yogyakarta/)

## Kayon / gunungan

The kayon retains a pointed mountain/leaf silhouette, central life-tree axis, and grounded base. Its use as the gate’s opening object is consistent with gunungan’s role as an opening, closing, and scene-transition marker.

Sources: [Indonesian Ministry of Culture — Gunungan philosophy](https://kebudayaan.kemdikbud.go.id/bpsmpsangiran/sarat-makna-budaya-ini-filosofi-gunungan-dalam-logo-presidensi-g20-indonesia/), [ISI Yogyakarta — Yogyakarta kayon form and function](https://digilib.isi.ac.id/3193/)

## General batik treatment

Batik carries symbolic meaning through its colors and designs and is used in life ceremonies including marriage. The digital treatment therefore uses motif fragments as respectful visual vocabulary, not as generic surface decoration.

Source: [UNESCO — Indonesian Batik](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/indonesian-batik-00170)
